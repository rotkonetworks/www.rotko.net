## what happened

At the era 2238 boundary the daily validator election ran, as it does every era, and dot01 was not
among the 600 elected. It has stayed out since. Rewards stop while a validator is out of the set:
no era points, no staker rewards, no self-stake incentive. Nothing is slashed, nothing is chilled,
the bond is untouched, and the node is healthy. It is simply not being used.

The obvious first assumption, the one we made out loud for an hour, was that a large nominator had
pulled its stake. It is the normal cause, and it is what happened to us on 16 July when a programme
de-nominated after an incident. So we checked. And the chain said we were wrong.

## what the chain actually says

We queried our own Polkadot Asset Hub archive node directly, no third-party explorer in the loop.
The method: take every nominator that was actually backing dot01 in era 2237, the last era we were
elected, and check each one's current nomination targets on-chain today.

    era 2237 backers:      65 nominators, 1,675,207 DOT
    still nominate us:     64 nominators, 1,674,112 DOT   (99.9%)
    actually dropped us:    1 nominator,      1,095 DOT   (0.07%)

Ninety-nine point nine percent of the stake that backed us still lists us. One small nominator, a
thousand DOT, removed us. Our support did not leave. Against a current election cutoff near 1.15M
DOT, 1.67M of standing intent should put us well inside the set.

So why are we out.

## how a validator can have the votes and still lose the seat

Polkadot does not simply sum the stake pointed at a validator. It runs a
[Phragmén](https://wiki.polkadot.network/docs/learn-phragmen) election that distributes each
nominator's stake across the validators they nominate, optimizing to make the *least-backed* elected
validator as well-backed as possible. That is good for network security. It also means the stake a
nominator points at you is not the stake you are assigned.

Our largest backer nominates three validators, us among them, with 1.4M DOT. In era 2237 the
election solution happened to assign the bulk of that to dot01. In era 2238 a shift elsewhere in the
global stake graph made the optimizer score better by pouring that nominator's weight into its other
two validators instead, and dot01's assigned exposure fell below the cutoff. The nominator did not
change anything. The optimizer did. On the next era, or any era, it can swing back just as
arbitrarily.

This is a known property of the system, not a bug and not a failure on anyone's part. But for a
validator whose backing is concentrated in a few large, multi-target nominators, it means active-set
membership is decided by an optimizer's tie-breaking, not by how much support you actually have. That
is a bad position to depend on, and it is ours today.

## the durable fix: stake we control

The lesson is blunt. Borrowed backing, even loyal borrowed backing, is allocated by math we do not
control. The way off that treadmill is stake the validator controls directly: self-stake. The
post-AHM economics reward it heavily. The validator self-stake incentive is a real, separate slice
of every era's issuance, distributed by the square root of self-stake up to a 30k DOT optimum, and it
is paid to our stash every era regardless of the election tie-breaking that governs nominator
allocation.

We are not going to solve this by asking supporters to nominate us more exclusively, which just
pushes the same fragile optimization around. We are building the thing that removes the dependency:
**rDOT, a contract-owned validator stash.**

The design, which has been in development and security review for weeks and is unrelated to this
week's incident except that this week proves why it matters:

- Supporters deposit DOT into a pool contract and receive rDOT, a liquid shares token, in return.
- The pool allocates that DOT into validator stashes that *are* smart contracts: keyless accounts
  whose only ability to act is through a fixed, whitelisted set of staking calls. There is no
  custody hatch, no arbitrary dispatch, no path for an operator to move principal anywhere but back
  to the pool or treasury.
- Each stash bonds the pooled DOT as its own self-stake. That converts supporter capital from
  fragile, optimizer-allocated nomination into hard self-stake that counts fully and earns the
  self-stake incentive, in a trust-minimized way where the contract, not us, holds the funds.

In other words: the same supporters who want to back dot01 can back it with capital that actually
lands where it is pointed, because the stash is a contract they can verify rather than a promise they
have to trust. That is the difference between 1.67M DOT of intent that an optimizer can scatter and
self-stake that cannot be reallocated away from us.
