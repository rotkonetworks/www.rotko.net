*Most RPKI mistakes are one field: **maxLength**. Get it wrong and your more-specific
prefixes are RPKI-invalid — silently discarded by every drop-invalid network — while the
covering aggregate keeps working and hides it. Here's how it works and how to check yours.*

---

## What maxLength actually means

A ROA (Route Origin Authorization) says: *"AS X may originate prefix P, for any
more-specific up to length L."* That `L` is **maxLength**.

If you publish a ROA for `2401:a860::/32` with **maxLength = /32**, then:

- `2401:a860::/32` from your AS → **valid**
- `2401:a860:1000::/40` from your AS → **invalid (`invalid_length`)** — longer than maxLength
- `2401:a860:1081::/48` from your AS → **invalid (`invalid_length`)**

RPKI origin validation doesn't care that *you* originate the more-specific. It's longer
than the ROA permits, so it's invalid. Networks running **drop-invalid** (a growing set —
AWS, many Tier-1s and IXPs) will **discard** it.

## Why it hides

Here's the trap: the covering aggregate is still valid. If you announce both
`2401:a860::/32` (valid) and a more-specific `/48` (invalid), traffic to an address inside
the `/48` still reaches you — via the `/32` — on the networks that dropped the `/48`. So
the prefix "works," but your **traffic-engineering / anycast-site more-specific silently
doesn't propagate** to the enforcing half of the internet. It looks intermittent and
route-dependent, which is exactly what makes it hard to spot.

## How to check yours

RIPEstat's RPKI validation endpoint tells you in one request:

```
https://stat.ripe.net/data/rpki-validation/data.json?resource=AS<your-asn>&prefix=<prefix>
```

Check *every length you actually announce*, not just the aggregate. If a more-specific
comes back `invalid` with reason `invalid_length`, your maxLength is too short.

## The rule

- **Set maxLength to the longest prefix you actually originate** — no shorter.
- **Never announce anything longer than your maxLength.** One wrong digit and that
  prefix is invalid everywhere ROV is enforced.
- **Keep the covering aggregate valid** as a safety net during any ROA change — validators
  refresh on their own timers (roughly 10–60 min + repository propagation), so a maxLength
  update is not instant.

We tripped exactly this — a `/32`-maxLength ROA while announcing `/48` and `/40`
more-specifics — and fixed it by publishing a ROA that covers the `/40` and originating
the RPKI-valid `/40`. Worth knowing: in our own incident this turned out to be a *latent
correctness bug*, not the user-visible symptom we were chasing (that was an IPv6 MTU
problem — [separate write-up](/news/2026-08-08-ipv6-tls-mss-pmtud-blackhole)). Two lessons
for the price of one: fix your RPKI maxLength regardless, and don't assume the first real
bug you find is the one causing your symptom.
